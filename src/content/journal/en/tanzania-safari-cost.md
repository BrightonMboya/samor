A safari quote usually arrives as one number per person. That is how we send ours too, because it is the number you actually plan around. But it helps to know what sits inside it, so you can see which parts are fixed and which ones you can change.

Here is how a private safari in northern Tanzania breaks down, roughly from the biggest share to the smallest.

## 1. Where you sleep

Lodges and tented camps are the largest part of almost every quote. On safari the nightly rate is usually **full board**: breakfast, lunch, dinner and often the game drives run by the lodge itself. That is why a safari night costs more than a hotel night in a city. You are paying for the room, every meal, and a building that has to be supplied by road across a national park.

The spread between lodges is wide. A well-run lodge outside the park gate and a small camp deep in the Serengeti can differ by several times per night, for the same animals on the same morning drive. We put you in the place that suits the day, not the most expensive one on the map.

## 2. Park and conservation fees

Every national park and the Ngorongoro Conservation Area charge a fee **per person, per 24 hours** inside the boundary. These are set by the government, not by us or by the lodge, and they are the same whoever you travel with.

Two things to know:

- **Staying inside a park costs more than staying just outside it**, because you pay the fee for every night you sleep there. A lodge on the rim of a park can save a noticeable amount on a long trip.
- **The Ngorongoro Crater has an extra charge per vehicle, per descent.** It is worth it. Most people only need to go down once.

Park fees change from time to time. We quote them at the current rate and tell you if they change before you travel.

## 3. Your vehicle and your guide

Every Samur safari is private. You have your own 4x4 with a pop-up roof, your own guide, and fuel for the whole route. This is charged **per vehicle, not per person**, which is why the price per person drops as your group grows. Two people and six people need the same car and the same guide.

For larger families we use two vehicles, so nobody sits in the middle seat for twelve days.

## 4. Flights inside Tanzania

Most of our trips drive the safari circuit and then fly from the Serengeti or Arusha to Zanzibar. Light aircraft flights are priced per seat and carry a **15 kg luggage limit** in a soft bag. If you would rather fly into the Serengeti and skip the long drive out, that adds a flight but saves you most of a day.

International flights to Tanzania are not in our price. Most of our guests book those themselves, and we can recommend routings.

## 5. Zanzibar

Beach hotels on Zanzibar are priced like hotels, usually per room with breakfast or half board, so the coast often costs less per night than the safari. Zanzibar also asks every visitor to buy **its own mandatory travel insurance** on arrival. We include that in the quote so you don't meet it at the airport.

## What is not in the price

- **International flights** to Kilimanjaro, Arusha or Zanzibar
- **Your Tanzania visa**, which you apply for yourself online (see our [visa guide for Saudi travellers](/en/journal/tanzania-visa-saudi-citizens))
- **Tips**: about USD 10–15 a day for your guide and USD 5 a day for lodge staff
- **Optional extras** such as the balloon flight over the Serengeti
- Personal travel and medical insurance

## The five choices that move the price most

1. **When you travel.** June to October and the last two weeks of December are peak season. In April and May many lodges drop their rates, and the parks are close to empty. Read more in [when to go](/en/journal/best-time-to-visit-tanzania).
2. **Which lodges you choose.** This is the biggest lever, and the easiest to change night by night.
3. **How many of you travel.** The vehicle and guide are shared across the group.
4. **How long you stay.** The first few days carry the fixed costs. Extra nights cost less each.
5. **Drive or fly.** Flying between parks saves time and costs more.
